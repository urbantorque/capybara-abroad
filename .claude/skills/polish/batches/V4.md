# V4 — The lens

**Est. 1–2 h.** The shader edit is twenty minutes. The rest is **re-validating
twenty grade rows**, because a tone-map changes what `threshold: 1.02` means in
every one of them.

**Run this last.** It re-grades every chapter, so it wants the ground, the
lights and the rim settled underneath it.

## Three findings, in priority order

### 1. The composite clamps instead of rolling off

`renderer.toneMapping = THREE.NoToneMapping`, and `MAIN_POST_COMP`'s first act
is `clamp(c, 0.0, 1.0)` with no filmic curve. The scene target is half-float, so
over-white values do survive as far as the bright pass — but the **visible**
pixel under a bloom is a hard-edged flat white with a hue shift toward whichever
primary clipped last.

Measured honestly: clipping in the ground band is **0% in all eight sampled
chapters**. This is not causing damage where the player is looking. It is a
roll-off and highlight-colour quality issue concentrated in skies, water and
emissives. Real, worth doing, and correctly ranked last.

Add an ACES-style filmic roll-off **before the sRGB encode** in
`MAIN_POST_COMP`.

**Then re-check every row of `sysGRADES`.** The per-chapter threshold reasoning
is load-bearing and hard-won — read the Venice and Antarctica comments before
touching either. A chapter built out of white stone cannot have a low threshold;
that argument does not change, but the *number* that expresses it does once
values roll off instead of clipping.

### 2. The split tone does not exist

The grade's `uTint` is a single global multiply and `uLift` is **zero in every
one of the twenty rows**. Monte Carlo's own comment states its intent as "warm
in the highlights and blue in the shadows" — a split tone. The shader has no
luminance-dependent term, so this cannot happen and does not.

Either implement a luminance-dependent split, or correct the comment. Do not
leave a comment describing behaviour the code cannot produce.

### 3. The sky is two colours and nothing else

`sysBuildSky` paints a dome from a horizon colour and a zenith colour on a
`t^0.62` ramp. There is no sun disc and no warmth around the sun's bearing —
so in every chapter the sun is an inference from the shadows rather than a thing
in the picture.

Add a sun disc and horizon glow, positioned from the existing `sun` directional
light so it cannot drift from the lighting. Keep it in the vertex colours if
possible — the dome is 1,100 vertices and one draw call, and it should stay that
way.
